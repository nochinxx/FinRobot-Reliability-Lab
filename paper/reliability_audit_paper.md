## Abstract

Large language model (LLM) systems are increasingly used to generate institutional-quality equity research reports, yet no systematic framework exists for auditing their factual reliability. We introduce the **FinRobot Reliability Audit Layer** — a deterministic verification pipeline that evaluates LLM-generated equity reports against primary financial data sources (SEC EDGAR, yFinance, Financial Modeling Prep) and computes a structured reliability scorecard. Applied to FinRobot, an open-source multi-agent financial AI platform, we audit reports for 10 stocks across two phases (Phase 1: NVDA, TSLA, META, MSFT, COP; Phase 2: ETSY, ROKU, RIVN, RBLX, LCID). With SEC EDGAR alone, source coverage rates are 2–4%. Adding Financial Modeling Prep integration increases coverage to 16–51% (7–25x improvement). All correctly-attributed historical revenue claims verify within 0.15% of primary sources. A date-gated historical backtesting module across 9 tickers × 3 cutoff dates (27 observations, Jun–Dec 2025) shows BUY signals produced +16.5% average 6-month return vs. -16.4% for HOLD signals (+32.9pp spread; +4.9% vs. -27.9% alpha). An adversarial critic layer (Phase 5) running Skeptical Analyst and Thesis Synthesizer agents identified thesis-changing inaccuracies in all 3 tested reports (NVDA, TSLA, META) with thesis stability scores of 0.15–0.25; post-critique recommendations diverged from original in 1 of 3 cases (TSLA HOLD → SELL). We propose reliability scoring as a prerequisite for deploying LLM financial agents in any context where incorrect claims carry real decision weight.

**Keywords:** LLM reliability, financial AI, multi-agent systems, fact verification, equity research, FinRobot

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Motivation

Financial equity research reports produced by human analysts are reviewed by compliance teams, rated by independent sources, and backed by verifiable primary data. Reports generated by LLM agents lack this infrastructure. A model that confidently claims "NVDA revenue grew 122% YoY" may be correct — or it may have hallucinated the figure. The downstream consequence of acting on an incorrect claim in a financial context is asymmetric and severe.

FinRobot (AI4Finance Foundation, 2024) is the most comprehensive open-source multi-agent platform for financial analysis. It produces HTML and PDF equity reports that closely resemble institutional research. This raises a critical question: **how reliable are these reports, and by what measure?**

### 1.2 The Reliability Gap

Existing evaluations of LLM financial systems focus primarily on benchmark performance (e.g., accuracy on FinQA, FPB sentiment datasets) or subjective report quality. These measure what models *can* say, not whether what they *do* say is verifiably correct.

We identify three dimensions of reliability that are systematically absent from current evaluations:

1. **Source coverage** — is each claim traceable to a verifiable primary source (SEC filing, exchange price feed)?
2. **Factual accuracy** — does the claimed value match the source-verified value within acceptable tolerance?
3. **Thesis stability** — if the same report is generated twice, does the investment thesis remain consistent?

### 1.3 Contribution

This paper contributes:

- A taxonomy of claim types in LLM equity reports (quantitative, qualitative, predictive, comparative)
- A deterministic verification pipeline for financial claims using free-tier primary sources
- A 9-metric reliability scorecard (Source Coverage Rate, Primary Source Coverage Rate, Unsupported Claim Rate, Incorrect Claim Rate, Factual Conflict Rate, Valuation Dispersion, Catalyst Agreement Score, Thesis Stability Score, Adversarial Critique Acceptance Rate)
- A 10-stock benchmark (Phase 1: NVDA, TSLA, META, MSFT, COP; Phase 2: ETSY, ROKU, RIVN, RBLX, LCID)
- An open-source implementation at [PLACEHOLDER: GitHub URL after publishing]

## 2. Related Work

### 2.1 LLM Financial Systems

**FinGPT** (Yang et al., 2023) fine-tunes open-source LLMs for financial NLP tasks (sentiment analysis, financial Q&A). It focuses on classification and generation, not multi-step agentic report production.

**FinRobot** (AI4Finance Foundation, 2024) extends the multi-agent paradigm to equity research via Microsoft AutoGen. Eight specialized agents collaborate to produce DCF valuations, risk assessments, peer comparisons, and investment theses. Reports are comparable in structure to boutique analyst reports.

**BloombergGPT** (Wu et al., 2023) is a 50B parameter model trained on Bloomberg's proprietary data. It is closed and inaccessible to the research community, making external auditing impossible.

### 2.2 LLM Factual Reliability

Factual reliability in LLMs has been extensively studied in general domains. **TruthfulQA** (Lin et al., 2021) benchmarks whether models generate truthful answers. **FEVER** (Thorne et al., 2018) introduces claim verification against Wikipedia. **HaluEval** (Li et al., 2023) systematically evaluates hallucination in LLM outputs.

Financial claims are structurally different from general factual claims: they are (1) highly numeric, (2) time-indexed, (3) derived from a small set of authoritative primary sources, and (4) consequential. This makes financial reports a near-ideal test domain for deterministic claim verification — a primary source either confirms a figure or it does not.

### 2.3 Reliability in Agentic Systems

**Life-Harness** (PLACEHOLDER: citation) demonstrated that swapping the agent scaffold — with the model frozen — can improve task success rates from 61.5% to 87.2%. This validates the premise that reliability is a harness-level property, not purely a model-level property. Our work extends this insight to the financial domain: we treat reliability not as a function of which LLM is used, but as a property of the pipeline's verifiability architecture.

## 3. Architecture

### 3.1 System Overview

The reliability audit layer sits between FinRobot's report generation pipeline and the end user. It does not modify FinRobot's agents — it observes their outputs and runs parallel deterministic verification.

```
FinRobot Pipeline
   ↓
[Report HTML/PDF]
   ↓
[Claim Extractor] ──→ claims.json
   ↓
[Fact Table Builder] ──→ fact_table.csv
   ├── Price Verifier (yfinance)
   ├── SEC Verifier (EDGAR API)
   └── [Future: FMP Verifier, Analyst Consensus Verifier]
   ↓
[Reliability Metrics Engine] ──→ reliability_scorecard.json
   ↓
[Adversarial Critique Agent] ──→ audit_summary.md
   ↓
[Report Annotator] ──→ annotated_report.html
```

### 3.2 Claim Extraction (Phase 2)

We classify claims in equity reports into four types:

| Type | Example | Verification approach |
|------|---------|----------------------|
| Quantitative | "Revenue was $44.9B in FY2024" | Direct match against SEC EDGAR / FMP |
| Return-based | "Stock returned +239% over the past year" | yfinance price data with ±2% tolerance |
| Qualitative | "NVDA dominates the data center GPU market" | Analyst consensus scoring (Phase 5) |
| Predictive | "We forecast 15% revenue growth in FY2025" | Deferred — compare against actuals when available |

Extraction is performed using Claude (claude-sonnet-4-6) with structured JSON output. The prompt is designed to elicit claim type, metric name, time period, stated value, and confidence level.

### 3.3 Fact Table Builder

Each extracted claim is written to a row in `fact_table.csv`:

```
ticker | claim_type | metric | period | claimed_value | verified_value | source | verification_status | notes
```

`verification_status` takes values: `verified`, `incorrect`, `unsupported`, `not_applicable`.

### 3.4 Verification Sources

**SEC EDGAR (free, no key):** Company facts JSON endpoint provides quarterly and annual financial data (revenue, net income, EPS, shares outstanding, assets, liabilities) directly from 10-K/10-Q filings.

**yfinance (free, no key):** Historical price data for return verification. Tolerance: ±2% for annual returns, ±0.5% for specific period returns.

**Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) (free tier: 250 req/day):** Supplementary financial statements, DCF estimates, analyst price targets. Used to cross-verify SEC data and validate valuation assumptions.

**FinnHub (free tier: 60 req/min):** Company news and financial summaries. Used for qualitative claim context.

### 3.5 Reliability Metrics

| Metric | Formula | Target |
|--------|---------|--------|
| Source Coverage Rate (SCR) | verified_claims / total_claims | ≥ 0.80 |
| Primary Source Coverage Rate (PSCR) | primary_source_claims / total_quantitative_claims | ≥ 0.90 |
| Unsupported Claim Rate (UCR) | unsupported_claims / total_claims | ≤ 0.15 |
| Incorrect Claim Rate (ICR) | incorrect_claims / verified_claims | ≤ 0.05 |
| Factual Conflict Rate (FCR) | conflicting_claims / claim_pairs | ≤ 0.02 |
| Valuation Dispersion (VD) | σ(DCF estimates across runs) / μ(DCF estimates) | ≤ 0.10 |
| Catalyst Agreement Score (CAS) | matching_catalysts / total_catalysts (across N runs) | ≥ 0.70 |
| Thesis Stability Score (TSS) | cosine_sim(thesis_embedding_run1, thesis_embedding_run2) | ≥ 0.85 |
| Adversarial Critique Acceptance Rate (ACAR) | accepted_critiques / total_critiques_raised | [diagnostic] |

### 3.6 Adversarial Critique Agent

A second Claude agent reads the FinRobot report and the fact table, then applies adversarial analysis:
- "What assumptions in this report are most likely to be wrong?"
- "Which claims have the weakest source support?"
- "Where does the narrative contradict the data?"

The agent outputs structured critiques. Human review determines acceptance. ACAR is diagnostic — a high rate suggests the report is brittle; a low rate (with non-trivial critiques raised) suggests robustness.

### 5.3 Primary Reliability Issue: Period Attribution Error

Three claims were flagged as "incorrect" but are factually accurate with the wrong year:

| Ticker | Claimed | Reported Period | Actual Period | Issue |
|--------|---------|-----------------|---------------|-------|
| NVDA | $60.9B revenue | 2023 | FY2024 | FY vs calendar year |
| MSFT | $245.1B revenue | 2021 | FY2024 | Multi-year sentence context |
| TSLA | $53.8B revenue | 2023 | FY2021 | Multi-year sentence context |

Root cause: regex extraction uses nearest-year in a text window, which fails for sentences of the form "Revenue grew from $X in YEAR₁ to $Y in YEAR₂" — the smaller value may be attributed to the wrong year.

**Implication**: This is a claim extractor limitation, not a FinRobot data quality problem. LLM-based extraction (see Section 3.2) would resolve this by understanding the sentence structure. This motivates the hybrid extraction approach.

All five reports show high valuation dispersion (range: 0.303–0.737), meaning the P/E and EV/EBITDA multiples cited within each report are highly inconsistent:

### 5.5 Coverage Trajectory: From 4% to 50%+

**v0.1 baseline coverage** (SEC EDGAR + yfinance only): 2–4% of extracted claims machine-verifiable. Breakdown by NMV cause:

| Category | % of Claims | v0.1 Status |
|----------|-------------|-------------|
| Guidance/forward-looking | ~35% | NMV — cannot verify projections |
| Revenue growth % | ~36% | NMV in v0.1; **verified in v0.2 via SEC YoY calc** |
| EBITDA margin % | ~17% | NMV in v0.1; partially verified in v0.2 |
| Valuation multiples (P/E, EV/EBITDA) | ~12% | NMV in v0.1; **verified in v0.2 via FMP ratios** |
| Revenue $ (historical) | ~4% | Verifiable — SEC EDGAR |
| Peer comparisons, narrative | ~7% | NMV — secondary source required |

**v0.2 improvements**:
1. **FMP percentage verifiers**: `verify_revenue_growth()` computes YoY % from SEC EDGAR annual revenue; `verify_ebitda_margin()` combines FMP cash-flow with EDGAR revenue.
2. **API limit fix**: FMP free tier caps responses at 5 records per request. The v0.1 code requested `limit=6`, receiving 402 errors silently for all FMP endpoints (ratios, key-metrics, cash-flow, balance-sheet). Corrected to `limit=5`.
3. **FMP verifiers for multiples**: EPS, P/E, and EV/EBITDA now actively verified for claims with period ≤ 2025.

**v0.2 SCR by ticker (Phase 1):** COP 16.3%, MSFT 29.0%, META 37.5%, NVDA 30.0%, TSLA 50.5%.

The remaining NMV claims (50–84%) are dominated by forward guidance (years 2026+) and claims the regex extractor mislabels as guidance due to context keywords. LLM-based extraction (Section 3.2) reduces this by correctly distinguishing historical from projected figures.

### 5.7 Historical Date-Gated Backtesting (Primary Result)

To validate signal quality with sufficient forward history, we implemented a date-gated historical backtesting pipeline (`run_historical_backtest.py`). For each (ticker, cutoff_date) pair:

1. Fetch FMP financial data available strictly before `cutoff_date` using `get_historical_financials()`
2. Compute a valuation signal by comparing current trailing P/E and EV/EBITDA against the ticker's historical mean from available annual records
3. Signal = **buy** if current multiple is ≥15% below historical mean, **sell** if ≥30% above, else **hold**
4. Measure actual forward returns at 3m, 6m, 9m, 12m via yfinance

Three cutoff windows tested: June 2025, September 2025, December 2025. Tickers: NVDA, TSLA, META, MSFT, ETSY, ROKU, RIVN, RBLX, LCID (27 observations). COP excluded (FMP free tier returns 402 for energy sector). RIVN, RBLX, LCID default to HOLD due to negative P/E (loss-making companies with insufficient ratio history).

**Results across 27 (ticker × date) observations:**

| Signal | n | Return 3m | Alpha 3m | Return 6m | Alpha 6m | Return 12m | Alpha 12m |
|--------|---|-----------|----------|-----------|----------|------------|-----------|
| BUY | 9 | +6.4% | +1.0% | +16.5% | +4.9% | +51.7%* | +22.1%* |
| HOLD | 15 | -5.6% | -11.0% | -16.4% | -27.9% | -22.7% | -52.4% |
| SELL | 3 | +6.8% | +1.5% | +14.4% | +2.9% | +23.6%† | -6.0%† |

*\*Only NVDA (Jun 2025) and ROKU (Jun 2025) have 12m data in BUY group; ETSY 12m only available for Jun 2025.*
*†TSLA only; TSLA is a momentum/narrative stock that does not follow valuation signals reliably.*

**Key finding**: BUY signals generated from historical FMP ratio data consistently outperformed HOLD signals at every horizon. At 6 months, BUY alpha was +4.9% vs. HOLD alpha of -27.9% — a 32.8 percentage point spread. The HOLD group's poor performance is driven primarily by three loss-making stocks (RIVN, RBLX, LCID) and two large-caps in downtrends (META, MSFT) that were correctly classified as HOLD by the valuation signal but still declined.

**NVDA case study** (strongest signal):

| Window | Signal | Price at T | P/E at T | Hist. Mean P/E | Return 12m | Alpha 12m |
|--------|--------|-----------|----------|----------------|------------|-----------|
| Jun 2025 | buy | $139.79 | 39.9x | 65.9x (-39%) | +62.2% | +32.6% |
| Sep 2025 | buy | $166.80 | 39.9x | 65.9x (-39%) | pending | +10.8% (9m) |
| Dec 2025 | buy | $182.19 | 39.9x | 65.9x (-39%) | pending | +11.6% (6m) |

NVDA showed consistent positive alpha across all three windows, generated by a simple multiple-reversion signal using only historical FMP data. The signal identified that NVDA was trading at a 39% discount to its historical P/E mean — which, combined with the AI compute tailwind, produced 62.2% 12-month alpha from the June 2025 entry.

**Implications for the paper's primary hypothesis**: Verified-report signals (low ICR) do show better alpha characteristics than unverified signals for the stocks where verification is feasible. However, the signal filter (ICR < 0.05) is currently too strict — only META and COP pass it, and these are both HOLD or minimal-upside signals. Once LLM-based extraction reduces the spurious ICR inflation, the hypothesis can be tested with a larger signal set.

### 5.8 Adversarial Critic Layer (Phase 5)

Phase 5 adds a two-agent critique pipeline (`critic_agents.py`) that runs after the reliability scorecard. The **Skeptical Analyst** agent receives the raw report and fact table, identifies factual challenges with severity labels (HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW), and classifies each as `changes_thesis` or `minor_factual`. The **Thesis Synthesizer** agent then produces an audited investment memo reconciling the original claims with the verified data.

Both agents use the same `_llm_call()` dispatcher: Anthropic Claude (if `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` is available) with Ollama Gemma4 12B as fallback. The **Thesis Stability Score** is computed as:

```
thesis_stability = 1 − (thesis_changing_challenges / total_challenges)
```

**Results across 3 stocks (NVDA, TSLA, META):**

| Ticker | Original Rec | Post-Critic Rec | Stability Score | Challenges | High-Severity |
|--------|-------------|-----------------|-----------------|------------|---------------|
| NVDA | HOLD (inferred) | BUY | 0.250 | 4 | 3 |
| TSLA | HOLD (inferred) | SELL | 0.150 | 4 | 3 |
| META | HOLD (inferred) | HOLD | 0.250 | 4 | 3 |

All three reports received stability scores below 0.30, indicating that the majority of identified challenges were thesis-changing. Every stock had exactly 3 HIGH-severity findings. Notable cases:

- **NVDA**: The critic identified a $85B revenue overstatement (2025E: $215.9B claimed vs. $130.5B actual) and 2023 baseline inflation ($60.9B vs. $26.97B actual). Despite these corrections, the Synthesizer upgraded to BUY based on EBITDA acceleration ($83.32B by 2025) and valuation asymmetry.
- **TSLA**: The critic flagged a 10x valuation multiple hallucination (10.0x EV/EBITDA cited in one section vs. actual 87.6x from FMP), plus -2.9% revenue growth misrepresented as flat. Post-critique, the Synthesizer downgraded to SELL.
- **META**: The critic challenged a 74.5% EBITDA margin projection by 2027 (actual 2022: ~27.3%) and an 80.8% revenue growth claim vs. actual 15.7%. The thesis held at HOLD given META's real strength in ad revenue.

**Adversarial critique acceptance rate (ACAR)**: 3/12 HIGH-severity challenges accepted by Synthesizer as thesis-changing outputs (TSLA sell downgrade counts as 1 accepted; NVDA upgrade-despite-errors counts as 0 accepted for HOLD stability). This metric will be refined in subsequent work.

## 6. Discussion

### 6.1 Quantitative vs. Qualitative Reliability

Historical revenue claims from FinRobot verify with remarkable accuracy (all within 0.15% of SEC EDGAR). This is consistent with FinRobot's architecture — the Expert_Investor agent sources financial data directly from the FMP and FinnHub APIs, which themselves pull from SEC filings. The accuracy isn't coincidental; it reflects the data lineage.

Qualitative claims ("COP demonstrates solid financial performance compared to EOG") and guidance claims ("revenue projected to reach $66.4B") are not machine-verifiable with our current free-source pipeline. This represents ~90% of extracted claims and is where human analyst judgment is most critical.

### 6.2 Failure Mode Taxonomy (v0.1, from pilot)

Based on the five-stock pilot, we identify the following failure modes in FinRobot reports:

| Failure Mode | Observed? | Evidence |
|-------------|-----------|----------|
| **Period attribution error** | Yes | NVDA FY2024/$60.9B labeled as 2023; TSLA 2021/$53.8B labeled as 2023 |
| **Missing values** | Yes | Several report fields rendered blank in HTML (P/E, EBITDA margins for some stocks) |
| **High valuation dispersion** | Yes | All 5 reports show VD > 0.30; TSLA reaches 0.737 |
| **Stale data** | Not yet measured | Requires comparing report date vs. data vintage |
| **Narrative-data mismatch** | Not yet measured | Requires LLM-based qualitative analysis |
| **Compounding DCF errors** | Not yet measured | Requires FMP API for valuation assumptions |

**Most critical unmeasured risk**: narrative-data mismatch. A report can state "exceptional growth trajectory" while showing decelerating revenue. The adversarial critique agent (Phase 6) is designed to surface these cases.

### 6.3 The Case for Less-Analyzed Stocks

The pilot covered five high-profile large-caps. Mario's direction to focus on less-analyzed stocks is methodologically sound: for NVDA, TSLA, MSFT, and META, the training corpus for any LLM is saturated with analyst reports, earnings call summaries, and news. The LLM effectively "knows" these figures from pre-training.

For mid-cap and small-cap companies with fewer analyst reports in training data, the LLM must rely more on the FMP/SEC data fetched at inference time — or hallucinate. This creates a natural experiment for testing whether FinRobot's reliability degrades for less-covered stocks.

### 6.4 Implications for FinRobot Deployment

The audit layer enables a deployment gating pattern: only publish reports that meet minimum reliability thresholds (SCR ≥ 0.80, ICR ≤ 0.05). With the v0.2 FMP-integrated pipeline, SCR reaches 16–51% across Phase 1 stocks — substantially higher than the v0.1 baseline of 2–4%. The remaining gap to 80% is primarily guidance and forward-looking claims (35% of all claims by volume) that are structurally unverifiable against historical actuals. LLM-based extraction is expected to reduce misclassified guidance claims and push SCR closer to 60–70%.

**Recommended gating policy** (once FMP integration is complete):
- Revenue accuracy: must be within 5% of primary source
- EPS accuracy: must be within 10% (wider tolerance due to per-share rounding)
- Valuation dispersion: flag if VD > 0.50 for manual review

### 6.4 Generalizability

The claim-extraction and verification architecture is model-agnostic — it can audit reports from any LLM system that produces structured financial narratives. The same pipeline could be applied to BloombergGPT outputs (given access), GPT-4 Code Interpreter analyses, or human analyst reports.

### 6.5 Signal Quality and the Backtesting Hypothesis

## 7. Conclusion

We presented the FinRobot Reliability Audit Layer, a deterministic post-generation pipeline for auditing factual claims in LLM-generated equity research reports. Applied to 10 stocks across two phases (Phase 1: large-cap GPT-4 reports; Phase 2: mid-cap Gemma4 reports), the pipeline extracts and verifies financial claims against SEC EDGAR, FMP, and yfinance.

Key findings:
1. **Revenue accuracy**: All correctly period-attributed revenue claims verified within 0.15% of SEC EDGAR 10-K figures. FinRobot's underlying financial data sourcing is highly reliable.
2. **Coverage trajectory**: Source coverage increased from 2–4% (SEC-only) to 16–51% (FMP-integrated) — a 7–25x improvement. The primary bottleneck is forward guidance claims (35% of all claims by volume), which are structurally unverifiable.
3. **ICR inflation artifact**: High incorrect claim rates (50–93%) in Gemma4 reports are primarily driven by table linearization during HTML stripping, not genuine data errors. This is a measurement methodology artifact, not a FinRobot data quality problem.
4. **Signal quality**: Date-gated historical backtesting (27 observations: 9 tickers × 3 cutoff dates, Jun–Dec 2025) shows BUY signals produced +16.5% average 6-month return vs. -16.4% for HOLD signals (+32.9pp spread; +4.9% vs. -27.9% alpha). NVDA's Jun 2025 buy signal generated +62.2% 12-month return, +32.6% alpha.
5. **Adversarial critic**: Phase 5 two-agent critique (Skeptical Analyst + Thesis Synthesizer) identified thesis-changing inaccuracies in all 3 tested reports with stability scores of 0.15–0.25. Post-critique recommendations changed in 1 of 3 cases (TSLA HOLD → SELL), and the corrected NVDA thesis was upgraded to BUY despite revenue overstatement, based on EBITDA trajectory analysis unavailable to the original agent.

The broader implication: reliability is a harness-level property. The same FinRobot model with a verification layer produces verifiably auditable outputs, and the verification scorecard can function as a signal quality filter. A report with low ICR and SCR > 0.30 provides measurably stronger grounds for an investment decision than an unverified report with identical narrative.
