Auditing LLM-Generated Financial Reports: A Reliability Framework for Multi-Agent Equity Research

Mario Illesca
Department of Computer Science, San Francisco State University
Contact: mariojillesca@gmail.com

Abstract

Large language model (LLM) systems are increasingly used to generate institutional-quality equity research reports, yet no systematic framework exists for auditing their factual reliability. We introduce the FinRobot Reliability Audit Layer — a deterministic verification pipeline that evaluates LLM-generated equity reports against primary financial data sources (SEC EDGAR, yFinance, Financial Modeling Prep) and computes a structured reliability scorecard. Applied to FinRobot, an open-source multi-agent financial AI platform, we audit 723 claims across 10 stocks in two phases (Phase 1: NVDA, TSLA, META, MSFT, COP; Phase 2: ETSY, ROKU, RIVN, RBLX, LCID). With SEC EDGAR alone, source coverage rates are 2–4%. Adding Financial Modeling Prep integration and an HTML table parser increases coverage to 16–56% (7–28× improvement). 37 revenue claims verified: GPT-4 reports within 0.12% mean error of SEC EDGAR; Gemma4 reports within 0.76% (rounding difference, not data error). A date-gated historical backtesting module across 9 tickers × 3 cutoff dates (27 observations, Jun–Dec 2025) shows BUY signals produced +16.5% average 6-month return vs. −16.4% for HOLD signals (+32.9pp spread; +4.9% vs. −27.9% alpha). A multi-agent critic panel (Phase 5) — comprising a Skeptical Analyst, Quant Risk Reviewer, and Model Risk Reviewer — identified thesis-changing inaccuracies in all 3 tested Phase 1 reports (thesis stability 0.15–0.25; TSLA downgraded HOLD→SELL post-critique). Phase 2 analysis reveals that high ICR in Gemma4-generated reports is not solely an extraction artifact: the HTML table parser surfaces genuine hallucinations in forward projections (EBITDA margins of 43–72% claimed vs. ~9% actual for ETSY/ROKU). Across 723 total claims, 13.6% verified against primary sources (60 LOCKED Tier 1, 38 PROVISIONAL Tier 3). We propose reliability scoring as a prerequisite for deploying LLM financial agents in any context where incorrect claims carry real decision weight.

Keywords: LLM reliability, financial AI, multi-agent systems, fact verification, equity research, FinRobot

1. Introduction

1.1 Motivation

Financial equity research reports produced by human analysts are reviewed by compliance teams, rated by independent sources, and backed by verifiable primary data. Reports generated by LLM agents lack this infrastructure. A model that confidently claims “NVDA revenue grew 122% YoY” may be correct — or it may have hallucinated the figure. The downstream consequence of acting on an incorrect claim in a financial context is asymmetric and severe.

FinRobot (AI4Finance Foundation, 2024) is the most comprehensive open-source multi-agent platform for financial analysis. It produces HTML and PDF equity reports that closely resemble institutional research. This raises a critical question: how reliable are these reports, and by what measure?

1.2 The Reliability Gap

Existing evaluations of LLM financial systems focus primarily on benchmark performance (e.g., accuracy on FinQA, FPB sentiment datasets) or subjective report quality. These measure what models can say, not whether what they do say is verifiably correct.

We identify three dimensions of reliability that are systematically absent from current evaluations:

  1. Source coverage — is each claim traceable to a verifiable primary source (SEC filing, exchange price feed)?
  2. Factual accuracy — does the claimed value match the source-verified value within acceptable tolerance?
  3. Thesis stability — if the same report is generated twice, does the investment thesis remain consistent?

1.3 Contribution

This paper contributes:

  • A taxonomy of eight claim types in LLM equity reports: financial, valuation, stock-price, guidance, peer-comparison, catalyst, risk, narrative
  • A deterministic verification pipeline for financial claims using free-tier primary sources (SEC EDGAR, FMP, yfinance)
  • A 5-metric reliability scorecard (Source Coverage Rate, Primary Source Coverage Rate, Unsupported Claim Rate, Incorrect Claim Rate, Valuation Dispersion) with statistical confidence intervals and significance tests
  • A gate decision framework (PASS / HUMAN_REVIEW / FAIL) and a three-agent critic panel (adversarial, quant-risk, model-risk) for institutional deployment readiness
  • A 10-stock benchmark (Phase 1: NVDA, TSLA, META, MSFT, COP; Phase 2: ETSY, ROKU, RIVN, RBLX, LCID) with a 27-observation date-gated historical backtest
  • An open-source implementation at https://github.com/nochinxx/FinRobot-Reliability-Lab

1.4 Paper Structure

Section 2 covers related work. Section 3 describes the audit architecture. Section 4 details the experiment design. Section 5 presents results. Section 6 discusses findings and failure modes. Section 7 concludes.

2. Related Work

2.1 LLM Financial Systems

FinGPT (Yang et al., 2023) fine-tunes open-source LLMs for financial NLP tasks (sentiment analysis, financial Q&A). It focuses on classification and generation, not multi-step agentic report production.

FinRobot (AI4Finance Foundation, 2024) extends the multi-agent paradigm to equity research via Microsoft AutoGen. Eight specialized agents collaborate to produce DCF valuations, risk assessments, peer comparisons, and investment theses. Reports are comparable in structure to boutique analyst reports.

BloombergGPT (Wu et al., 2023) is a 50B parameter model trained on Bloomberg’s proprietary data. It is closed and inaccessible to the research community, making external auditing impossible.

2.2 LLM Factual Reliability

Factual reliability in LLMs has been extensively studied in general domains. TruthfulQA (Lin et al., 2021) benchmarks whether models generate truthful answers. FEVER (Thorne et al., 2018) introduces claim verification against Wikipedia. HaluEval (Li et al., 2023) systematically evaluates hallucination in LLM outputs.

Financial claims are structurally different from general factual claims: they are (1) highly numeric, (2) time-indexed, (3) derived from a small set of authoritative primary sources, and (4) consequential. This makes financial reports a near-ideal test domain for deterministic claim verification — a primary source either confirms a figure or it does not.

2.3 Reliability in Agentic Systems

Prior work on agentic scaffolding has demonstrated that pipeline architecture, independent of the underlying model, is a primary driver of task reliability. Our work applies this insight to the financial domain: we treat reliability not as a function of which LLM is used, but as a property of the pipeline’s verifiability architecture.

3. Architecture

3.1 System Overview

The reliability audit layer sits between FinRobot’s report generation pipeline and the end user. It does not modify FinRobot’s agents — it observes their outputs and runs parallel deterministic verification.

FinRobot Pipeline
   ↓
[Report HTML]
   ↓
[Claim Extractor]  ─── regex + HTML table parser + LLM ──→  claims.json
   ↓
[Fact Table Builder]  ─── SOURCE_CONFLICT detection ──→  fact_table.csv
   ├── SEC EDGAR Verifier  (Tier 1: revenue, EBITDA)
   ├── FMP Verifier        (Tier 3: EPS, P/E, EV/EBITDA, margins, FCF)
   └── Price Verifier      (yfinance: returns)
   ↓
[Reliability Scorecard]  ──→  reliability_scorecard.json
   ↓
[Gate Decision]  ──→  gate_decision.json   (PASS / HUMAN_REVIEW / FAIL)
   ↓
[Annotated HTML Report]  ──→  annotated_report.html
   ↓
[Critic Panel]
   ├── Phase 5a: Skeptical Analyst + Thesis Synthesizer  ──→  critic_findings.json
   ├── Phase 5b: Quant Risk Reviewer  ──→  quant_risk_review.json
   └── Phase 5c: Model Risk Reviewer  ──→  model_risk_review.json
   ↓
[Investment Committee Memo]  ──→  {TICKER}_investment_committee_memo.md

3.2 Claim Extraction (Phase 1)

We classify claims in equity reports into eight types: financial, valuation, stock-price, guidance, peer-comparison, catalyst, risk, narrative.

Extraction method Description Best for
regex Deterministic pattern matching on stripped HTML text Fast, reproducible, no API cost
LLM Claude (claude-sonnet-4-6) structured JSON output Handles complex multi-year sentences
table_parser Structural <table> parsing preserving column headers Gemma4/short-narrative reports

All three methods run by default: regex and table_parser always; LLM when ANTHROPIC_API_KEY is set. Table-parsed claims supplement regex/LLM claims for any (metric, period) pair not already covered, preventing double-counting.

3.3 Fact Table Builder + SOURCE_CONFLICT Detection

Each claim produces one row in fact_table.csv. For metrics with both a Tier 1 and Tier 3 verifier (currently EBITDA: SEC EDGAR op.income+D&A vs FMP net_income+D&A), both verifiers run and the results are compared:

  • Within tolerance: Tier 1 value recorded; source_tier=1, verification_status=verified
  • Beyond tolerance: verification_status=SOURCE_CONFLICT; both values recorded (verified_value, verified_value_alt); conflict_details JSON captures the delta

Tier 1 always wins for the primary value. The conflict is flagged for human review.

3.4 Verification Sources

Source Tier Key Metrics covered
SEC EDGAR XBRL 1 None Revenue, EBITDA proxy (op.income+D&A)
yfinance 1 None Price returns
FMP free tier 3 250 req/day EPS, P/E, EV/EBITDA, gross/net/EBITDA margins, FCF, total debt, market cap

When Tier 1 and Tier 3 values disagree beyond tolerance: SOURCE_CONFLICT recorded, Tier 1 wins.

3.5 Reliability Metrics

Five metrics are computed and scored against targets:

Metric Formula Target Gate condition
SCR (Source Coverage Rate) (verified + incorrect) / total ≥ 0.80 FAIL if < 0.10
PSCR (Primary Source Coverage) verified / machine-verifiable ≥ 0.90 —
UCR (Unsupported Claim Rate) unsupported / total ≤ 0.15 —
ICR (Incorrect Claim Rate) incorrect / machine-verifiable ≤ 0.05 FAIL if > 0.15
VD (Valuation Dispersion) σ / μ of valuation multiples ≤ 0.10 HUMAN_REVIEW if > 0.10

Gate policy: FAIL if ICR > 0.15 or SCR < 0.10 or SOURCE_CONFLICT ratio > 20%. HUMAN_REVIEW if any target fails or any SOURCE_CONFLICT present.

3.6 Critic Panel (Phase 5)

Three LLM-based agents run when --critic is passed:

Phase 5a — Adversarial Critic (critic_agents.py): Skeptical Analyst identifies 3–5 weakest claims with severity labels; Thesis Synthesizer produces an audited investment memo. Thesis Stability Score = 1 − (thesis-changing challenges / total challenges). Uses Anthropic Claude (temperature=0) with Ollama Gemma4 12B fallback.

Phase 5b — Quant Risk Reviewer (critics/quant_risk_reviewer.py): Assesses ICR as artifact vs. true error, identifies coverage gaps by metric, computes source-tier breakdown. Verdict: low / medium / high.

Phase 5c — Model Risk Reviewer (critics/model_risk_reviewer.py): Assesses determinism risk (temperature, provider), input sensitivity (context window heuristics), prompt fragility. Verdict: not-ready / prototype / conditional-production / production-ready.

All critic calls log provenance via run_metadata.jsonl (model name, provider, temperature, prompt version, prompt hash, input/output hashes).

4. Experiment Design

4.1 Five-Stock Phase 1 Pilot

Phase 1 audits five large-cap US equities using reports generated by the FinRobot repository’s default GPT-4-based agent pipeline:

Stock Sector Rationale
NVDA Semiconductors Highest analyst coverage; AI narrative creates hallucination pressure
TSLA EV/Auto Extreme valuation dispersion; frequent guidance misses — stress test for ICR
META Social Media Post-restructuring; clean revenue story makes SEC verification tractable
MSFT Cloud/Software Stable large-cap; tests FY vs calendar year attribution
COP Energy Commodity-linked; high revenue volatility; tests tolerance calibration

One report per ticker. Audit run using --mode regex for determinism; LLM extraction available via --mode auto.

4.2 Five-Stock Phase 2 Benchmark (Mid-Cap)

Phase 2 adds five mid-cap equities with reports generated by a locally-hosted Gemma4 12B MLX model (via Ollama), testing reliability where training data is sparser:

Stock Sector Key characteristic
ETSY E-commerce Niche marketplace; lower analyst coverage than Phase 1
ROKU Streaming Platform revenue model; ad dependency
RIVN EV Startup Pre-profitability; loss-making; FMP ratio history sparse
RBLX Gaming/Metaverse Bookings vs. revenue distinction creates extraction difficulty
LCID EV Startup Ultra-low revenue; tests near-zero baseline handling

Phase 2 introduces a confound: Gemma4 generates shorter narrative sections, concentrating numeric data in HTML tables — triggering the table-linearization artifact analyzed in Section 5.6.

4.3 Historical Backtest Design

A date-gated backtesting module (run_historical_backtest.py) generates valuation signals using only FMP financial data available strictly before a cutoff_date. Signal computation:

  1. Fetch FMP ratios/key-metrics available before cutoff_date
  2. Compute trailing P/E and EV/EBITDA vs. ticker’s historical mean from available annual records
  3. Signal = buy if current multiple ≥15% below historical mean; sell if ≥30% above; else hold
  4. Measure actual forward returns at 3m/6m/9m/12m via yfinance vs. SPY benchmark

Three cutoff windows tested: 2025-06-01, 2025-09-01, 2025-12-01. Nine tickers × 3 dates = 27 observations. RIVN, RBLX, LCID default to HOLD (negative P/E, insufficient ratio history). Statistical significance evaluated via Mann-Whitney U test and bootstrap confidence intervals (see Section 5.7).

4.4 Evaluation Protocol

For each ticker:

run_reliability_audit.py --ticker {TICKER} --mode auto --gate --annotate
  1. Phase 1: Claim extraction (regex + HTML table parser; LLM when API key available)
  2. Phase 2: Fact verification against SEC EDGAR (Tier 1) + FMP free tier (Tier 3)
  3. Phase 3: Reliability scorecard (5 metrics + gate decision)
  4. Phase 4b: Annotated HTML report
  5. Phase 5: Critic panel (requires --critic): adversarial, quant-risk, model-risk

All outputs in output/{TICKER}/. Aggregate: run_master_fact_table.py produces output/master_fact_table.csv.

5.3 Period Attribution and the ICR Inflation Artifact

Early pipeline versions (v0.1–v0.2) showed that several “incorrect” claims were factually accurate values attributed to the wrong year. Canonical examples:

Ticker Claimed Reported Period Actual Period Issue
NVDA $60.9B revenue 2023 FY2024 FY vs calendar year
MSFT $245.1B revenue 2021 FY2024 Multi-year sentence context
TSLA $53.8B revenue 2023 FY2021 Multi-year sentence context

Root cause: regex extraction uses nearest-year in a ±200-character text window, which fails for sentences of the form “Revenue grew from $X in YEAR₁ to $Y in YEAR₂” — the earlier value may absorb the later year’s label.

Fix (PR2): _nearest_year() now splits compound sentences on ” to ” / ” from ” boundaries before running year search. The three examples above now extract correctly, and the ICR inflation artifact for revenue levels is resolved.

Residual issue (PR6 finding): Period attribution for revenue_growth and ebitda_margin metrics remains challenging in v0.4, because these metrics require computing YoY % from two annual records — and the report may have used a different base year or different annual data source than SEC EDGAR. This drives 28% of incorrect claims (see Section 5.9 decomposition). The revenue level claims are now highly accurate; the rate claims require additional methodology work.

5.7 Historical Date-Gated Backtesting (Primary Result)

To validate signal quality with sufficient forward history, we implemented a date-gated historical backtesting pipeline (run_historical_backtest.py). For each (ticker, cutoff_date) pair:

  1. Fetch FMP financial data available strictly before cutoff_date using get_historical_financials()
  2. Compute a valuation signal by comparing trailing P/E and EV/EBITDA against the ticker’s historical mean from all available annual records before the cutoff
  3. Signal = buy if current multiple ≥15% below historical mean; sell if ≥30% above; else hold
  4. Measure actual forward returns at 3m, 6m, 9m, 12m via yfinance vs. SPY benchmark

Three cutoff windows: 2025-06-01, 2025-09-01, 2025-12-01. Nine tickers × 3 dates = 27 observations. COP excluded (FMP 402 error for energy sector on free tier). RIVN, RBLX, LCID default to HOLD (negative P/E, insufficient ratio history for mean computation).

Mann-Whitney U is the primary test: it makes no normality assumption, appropriate for n<30 and non-symmetric distributions. Both tests agree: the BUY/HOLD separation is unlikely to be sampling noise.

Information ratios (6m alpha consistency): - BUY: IR = 0.35 (positive, consistent outperformance) - HOLD: IR = −1.17 (consistent underperformance)

The HOLD group’s negative IR reflects a systematic pattern: stocks classified as HOLD by the valuation signal predominantly declined over 2025–2026 (three loss-making EV startups + two large-caps in macro-driven downtrends). The valuation signal correctly withheld endorsement from these names.

5.7.3 Important Caveats

  1. Sample size: n=27 total (9 BUY, 15 HOLD, 3 SELL). Statistical significance is established, but the effect may not generalize outside these tickers/windows.
  2. Signal family: This is a valuation-multiple mean-reversion signal (Family 1), not FinRobot’s AI recommendation (Family 2). The two are tracked separately and should not be conflated.
  3. Survivorship / selection: All 9 tickers were chosen before the backtest. No look-ahead bias, but the universe is limited to stocks the audit pipeline supports.
  4. SELL group: n=3 (TSLA across 3 windows). TSLA is a momentum stock that does not follow simple valuation signals — the SELL signal on TSLA is not statistically meaningful on its own.

NVDA case study (strongest signal):

Window Signal Price at T P/E at T Hist. Mean P/E Return 12m Alpha 12m
Jun 2025 buy $139.79 39.9x 65.9x (-39%) +62.2% +32.6%
Sep 2025 buy $166.80 39.9x 65.9x (-39%) pending +10.8% (9m)

NVDA showed consistent positive alpha across all three windows, generated by a simple multiple-reversion signal using only historical FMP data. The signal identified that NVDA was trading at a 39% discount to its historical P/E mean — which, combined with the AI compute tailwind, produced 62.2% 12-month alpha from the June 2025 entry.

Implications for the paper’s primary hypothesis: Verified-report signals (low ICR) do show better alpha characteristics than unverified signals for the stocks where verification is feasible. However, the signal filter (ICR < 0.05) is currently too strict — only META and COP pass it, and these are both HOLD or minimal-upside signals. Once LLM-based extraction reduces the spurious ICR inflation, the hypothesis can be tested with a larger signal set.

5.8 Adversarial Critic Layer (Phase 5)

Phase 5 adds a two-agent critique pipeline (critic_agents.py) that runs after the reliability scorecard. The Skeptical Analyst agent receives the raw report and fact table, identifies factual challenges with severity labels (HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW), and classifies each as changes_thesis or minor_factual. The Thesis Synthesizer agent then produces an audited investment memo reconciling the original claims with the verified data.

Both agents use the same _llm_call() dispatcher: Anthropic Claude (if ANTHROPIC_API_KEY is available) with Ollama Gemma4 12B as fallback. The Thesis Stability Score is computed as:

thesis_stability = 1 − (thesis_changing_challenges / total_challenges)

Results across 3 stocks (NVDA, TSLA, META):

Ticker Original Rec Post-Critic Rec Stability Score Challenges High-Severity
NVDA HOLD (inferred) BUY 0.250 4 3
TSLA HOLD (inferred) SELL 0.150 4 3
META HOLD (inferred) HOLD 0.250 4 3

All three reports received stability scores below 0.30, indicating that the majority of identified challenges were thesis-changing. Every stock had exactly 3 HIGH-severity findings. Notable cases:

  • NVDA: The critic identified a $85B revenue overstatement (2025E: $215.9B claimed vs. $130.5B actual) and 2023 baseline inflation ($60.9B vs. $26.97B actual). Despite these corrections, the Synthesizer upgraded to BUY based on EBITDA acceleration ($83.32B by 2025) and valuation asymmetry.
  • TSLA: The critic flagged a 10x valuation multiple hallucination (10.0x EV/EBITDA cited in one section vs. actual 87.6x from FMP), plus -2.9% revenue growth misrepresented as flat. Post-critique, the Synthesizer downgraded to SELL.
  • META: The critic challenged a 74.5% EBITDA margin projection by 2027 (actual 2022: ~27.3%) and an 80.8% revenue growth claim vs. actual 15.7%. The thesis held at HOLD given META’s real strength in ad revenue.

Adversarial critique acceptance rate (ACAR): 3/12 HIGH-severity challenges accepted by Synthesizer as thesis-changing outputs (TSLA sell downgrade counts as 1 accepted; NVDA upgrade-despite-errors counts as 0 accepted for HOLD stability). This metric will be refined in subsequent work.

5.9 ICR Source Decomposition

The high incorrect claim rates (ICR 0.36–1.00) across all 10 tickers warrant decomposition. We classify each of the 145 incorrect claims across the 10-ticker benchmark into three categories (reliability_lab/statistics/audit_stats.py):

Category Count % of Incorrect Description
Forward projection 71 49% FY2025 projections now verifiable against actual 2025 data
Growth misattribution 40 28% revenue_growth/ebitda_margin at FY2021–22; YoY methodology mismatch
Genuine error 34 23% Incorrect claims on verifiable historical figures

Implication: only 23% of “incorrect” claims reflect true data quality failures in FinRobot reports. 49% are forward projections that have become verifiable with the passage of time — these are not hallucinations at the time of generation. 28% are an artifact of how the verify_revenue_growth() function computes YoY % from SEC annual records, which can diverge from the growth rate the report computed using FMP data.

The adjusted ICR (genuine errors only / MV claims) across Phase 1 tickers: NVDA 0.083, TSLA 0.190, META 0.163, MSFT 0.214, COP 0.533. COP is the genuine outlier — its revenue volatility (commodity-linked) makes YoY growth claims inherently harder to verify within tolerance.

This decomposition is critical for interpreting the headline ICR numbers: a report with ICR=0.70 does not mean 70% of its verifiable claims are wrong — it means 70% don’t match primary sources in this pipeline, primarily because forward projections are now being tested against actuals that postdate the report.

5.10 ICR → Alpha Hypothesis Test

A natural question is whether audit quality predicts signal quality: do tickers with lower ICR (fewer incorrect claims) produce better alpha?

We test this using 27 (ticker × date) observations, assigning each observation the ticker’s ICR from the latest v0.4 audit run. Spearman rank correlation between ICR and 6-month alpha:

Scope Spearman ρ p-value n Interpretation
All observations −0.183 0.361 27 Near-zero, not significant
Within BUY signals +0.158 0.685 9 Near-zero, not significant
Within HOLD signals −0.178 0.529 15 Near-zero, not significant

The ICR–alpha correlation is not statistically significant at any scope. The primary confound is the valuation signal: tickers with high ICR (RIVN, RBLX, LCID: ICR=0.47–1.00) are all HOLD signals due to negative P/E, and the HOLD signal itself predicts poor returns. The ICR and the signal are correlated with each other, making it impossible to isolate the ICR effect without controlling for signal.

The ICR quartile pattern is directionally consistent: Q1 (lowest ICR: NVDA, RIVN) mean alpha = +3.7%; Q3 (ICR 0.67–0.71: LCID, MSFT) mean alpha = −38.1%. But this is driven by the signal, not the audit quality per se.

Conclusion: The hypothesis that lower ICR predicts better alpha is not confirmed at this scale. It remains plausible but would require: (1) a larger ticker universe with the same signal group (all BUY, varied ICR), (2) ICR measured at the time of signal generation (not the current audit), or (3) a two-stage analysis where ICR filters which signals to act on.

6. Discussion

6.1 Quantitative vs. Qualitative Reliability

Across 37 verified revenue claims in the 10-ticker benchmark, both GPT-4 and Gemma4 reports are quantitatively accurate on historical revenue: GPT-4 mean error 0.12% (all within 1%), Gemma4 mean error 0.76% (all within 2%). See Section 5.2. This is consistent with FinRobot’s architecture — the Expert_Investor agent sources financial data directly from FMP and SEC EDGAR APIs. The accuracy reflects the data lineage, not model memorization.

Where the two generators diverge is not in what data they report but how they represent and project it: GPT-4 states historical figures to the cent; Gemma4 rounds to the nearest $0.1B. More significantly, forward projections (FY2025+) diverge materially from actuals for Gemma4 — EBITDA margins of 43–72% were projected for ETSY/ROKU vs. ~9% actual. GPT-4 Phase 1 reports also contain forward projection errors (49% of all incorrect claims are FY2025 projections), but with narrower magnitude.

Qualitative claims (“COP demonstrates solid financial performance compared to EOG”) and guidance claims (“revenue projected to reach $66.4B”) remain structurally unverifiable against free-tier primary sources. These represent ~65% of extracted claims in the v0.4 pipeline — the primary bottleneck to reaching the SCR ≥ 0.80 deployment threshold.

6.2 Failure Mode Taxonomy (v0.4, 10-ticker benchmark)

Table linearization ✅ Fixed (PR3/PR6) HTML table parser preserves column–row structure; Phase 2 MV coverage 2→6–17 claims/ticker

Key shift from v0.1: The failure mode previously labeled “period attribution error” has been decomposed into two independent causes with different fixes — text-based misattribution (resolved by _nearest_year() fix) and table-linearization (resolved by the HTML table parser). These were conflated in v0.1.

The remaining high-leverage open problem is Tier 2 source integration. Company IR filings (earnings transcripts, press releases) would likely cover 60–70% of the currently-unverifiable guidance claims, moving aggregate SCR from 13.6% toward the 80% deployment threshold. This requires structured parsing of earnings call transcripts, which are freely available via SEC EDGAR Form 8-K.

6.3 Generator Dependence and the Coverage Asymmetry

The comparison between Phase 1 (GPT-4) and Phase 2 (Gemma4 12B) reveals a coverage asymmetry driven by output verbosity:

  • GPT-4 reports: ~2,000-character narrative sections with year-anchored sentences → regex extractor captures 30–58 MV claims/report
  • Gemma4 reports: ~300-character narratives with most numeric data in HTML tables → regex alone captures 1–2 MV claims; table parser raises this to 6–17

This asymmetry has a direct implication: the audit pipeline’s sensitivity scales with the LLM’s verbosity. A more compact generator (e.g., a fine-tuned 7B model) would produce fewer narrative claims, reducing regex coverage. The HTML table parser partially compensates, but the ideal extractor combines structural parsing (for tables) with sentence-level understanding (for narrative).

The Gemma4 12B results also confirm that the underlying financial data is model-independent: where Gemma4 does state historical revenue figures, they verify within 2% of SEC EDGAR — the same accuracy as GPT-4. The failure is in the forward projections, not the historical facts.

6.4 Deployment Roadmap

The audit layer implements a gating pattern: reports that fail minimum reliability thresholds are blocked from downstream use. The current pipeline produces FAIL or HUMAN_REVIEW for all 10 benchmark tickers — correct behavior, since no ticker meets the SCR ≥ 0.80 target. The following table maps each gap to its closure path:

Gap Current State Closure Path Estimated SCR Impact
Forward guidance (35% of claims) Structurally unverifiable LLM extraction distinguishing historical from forward; accept NMV +0pp (correctly NMV)
Revenue growth rates (10% of claims) YoY methodology mismatch Standardize base-year convention; Tier 2 earnings transcripts +5pp
Valuation multiples (12% of claims) FMP free tier (Tier 3) FMP paid tier or direct exchange feeds +3pp
Peer comparisons (7% of claims) No verifier Peer data from SEC EDGAR cross-reference +4pp
Guidance claims (20% of claims) Unverifiable vs. actuals Compare at T+12m; build retrospective verifier +8pp (deferred)
Narrative quality (~10%) Not verifiable by number-matching LLM-based semantic consistency check Diagnostic only

Quantifying Tier 2 impact: Of the 723 total claims across 10 tickers, 475 (65.7%) are currently unverifiable. Breaking down those 475 NMV claims: approximately 253 (35% of total) are forward guidance or projections; 51 (7%) are peer comparisons requiring cross-ticker data; and 171 (24%) are methodology-dependent (growth rates, narrative quality) or structurally ambiguous (rounded values, qualitative language). SEC EDGAR Form 8-K filings — which contain quarterly earnings releases and are freely available — would enable temporal retroactive verification: a guidance claim in a report dated T−12m can be checked against the T-period actual reported in the subsequent 8-K. This retroactive pipeline would unlock approximately 35pp of SCR (253/723), raising aggregate SCR from 13.6% to ~49%. Combined with standardized growth-rate methodology (+5pp) and peer-comparison cross-referencing (+4pp), an achievable medium-term SCR target is ~58% without paid data. Reaching ≥80% requires either a paid Tier 2 data feed (Bloomberg, Refinitiv) for full guidance coverage, or an explicit policy decision to exclude forward projections from the verifiable-claim universe — which would set the practical ceiling at 65% (100% minus the structurally-forward 35%).

The realistic near-term target is SCR ≈ 40–50% by adding earnings transcript parsing (SEC EDGAR 8-K filings) and standardizing the revenue growth base-year convention.

Implemented gating policy (reliability_lab/scoring/gates.py):

Decision Condition
FAIL ICR > 0.15 OR SCR < 0.10 OR SOURCE_CONFLICT ratio > 20%
HUMAN_REVIEW Any threshold fails OR any SOURCE_CONFLICT present
PASS SCR ≥ 0.80, PSCR ≥ 0.90, UCR ≤ 0.15, ICR ≤ 0.05, VD ≤ 0.10

For a model-risk deployment, we recommend treating the HUMAN_REVIEW decision not as “reject” but as “route to analyst desk” — the report is surfaced with a structured critique (from the Phase 5 critic panel) rather than suppressed. This preserves the value of FinRobot’s qualitative analysis while flagging the quantitative claims that require human verification.

Production-autonomous deployment (no human in the loop) requires all thresholds to be met simultaneously. Based on the roadmap above, the earliest realistic date for analyst-augmented deployment of Phase 1 tickers is Q3 2026 (pending Tier 2 integration); full-automation deployment requires a larger prospective study and is not on a near-term timeline.

6.5 Generalizability

The claim-extraction and verification architecture is model-agnostic and can audit reports from any LLM pipeline that produces structured financial narratives. Three dimensions of generalizability:

Generator agnosticism: The pipeline has been validated on GPT-4 (via FinRobot’s default agent) and Gemma4 12B (via Ollama). The HTML table parser handles both generators’ output formats. Any LLM that produces HTML reports with <table> elements and narrative text can be audited with no changes to the pipeline.

Sector coverage: The verifier suite covers the most common financial metrics across all equity sectors (revenue, EBITDA, EPS, P/E, EV/EBITDA, margins, FCF, total debt). Sector-specific metrics (e.g., book value for banks, same-store-sales for retail, proved reserves for energy) would require additional FMP or SEC EDGAR concept mappings in fact_table_builder.py.

Human analyst reports: The same pipeline could be applied to human-authored analyst reports, functioning as a fact-checking layer rather than an LLM audit. The ICR for human reports would serve as a baseline for comparison — expected to be lower than LLM-generated reports, but not zero (analysts also make period attribution errors and forward projection errors).

6.6 Signal Quality and the Backtesting Hypothesis

The reliability metrics (ICR, SCR, VD) can be used not only to flag untrustworthy reports but also to filter investment signals. We propose the following testable hypothesis:

Hypothesis: FinRobot “buy” signals with ICR < 0.05 and VD < 0.40 produce higher alpha at T+6m and T+1y than signals with ICR ≥ 0.10 or VD ≥ 0.50.

6.7 Limitations

We explicitly acknowledge the following limitations of this work:

Statistical: 1. Small sample: 27 backtest observations (9 BUY, 15 HOLD, 3 SELL). Although the BUY vs. HOLD separation is statistically significant (p=0.0035), effect sizes from small samples tend to be inflated (Winner’s Curse). Results should be treated as preliminary pending a larger replication. 2. Single-factor signal: The valuation signal is P/E mean-reversion only, not a multi-factor model. It is intentionally simple to isolate signal quality from audit quality, but it may not generalize. 3. Confounded ICR-alpha test: As shown in Section 5.10, the ICR-alpha correlation cannot be isolated from the valuation signal at this sample size. The hypothesis remains untested in the controlled sense.

Pipeline: 4. Coverage ceiling: At 13.6% verified claims across 10 tickers, the audit cannot confirm the majority of a report’s content. 65.7% of claims are structurally unverifiable (forward guidance, peer comparisons, narrative) with the current free-tier source architecture. 5. FMP free tier: 250 calls/day limits batch processing. All 10 tickers can be processed in one session, but scaling to 100+ tickers would require a paid plan. 6. LLM nondeterminism: Despite temperature=0, LLM extraction may vary across model versions. All results in this paper used Ollama Gemma4 12B for report generation and Claude claude-sonnet-4-6 for extraction (when API key available) with explicit run_metadata logging for reproducibility. 7. Single report per ticker: Results reflect one FinRobot report per ticker. Multiple runs per ticker would measure report variance, which is unmeasured here.

Scope: 8. FinRobot-specific: The pipeline is designed for FinRobot’s HTML output format. Adapting to other LLM report generators (BloombergGPT, GPT-4 Code Interpreter) would require format-specific parsers. 9. US equities only: SEC EDGAR coverage is US-only. International equities would require different Tier 1 sources (SEDAR, EDGAR International, etc.).

7. Conclusion

We presented the FinRobot Reliability Audit Layer, a deterministic post-generation pipeline for auditing factual claims in LLM-generated equity research reports. Applied to 10 stocks across two phases (Phase 1: large-cap GPT-4 reports; Phase 2: mid-cap Gemma4 reports), the pipeline extracts and verifies 723 financial claims against SEC EDGAR, FMP, and yfinance.

Key findings: 1. Revenue accuracy: 37 revenue claims verified across 10 tickers. GPT-4 reports: mean error 0.12% (all within 1%); Gemma4 reports: mean error 0.76% (all within 2%). FinRobot’s underlying financial data sourcing is reliable across both generators — differences in precision are representational, not accuracy failures.

  1. Coverage trajectory: Source coverage increased from 2–4% (SEC-only) to 16–56% (FMP-integrated + HTML table parser) — a 7–28× improvement. 13.6% of all 723 claims verified against primary sources (60 LOCKED Tier 1, 38 PROVISIONAL Tier 3). The remaining 65.7% are forward guidance or structurally unverifiable claims.

  2. ICR is partially an artifact, partially genuine: High incorrect claim rates (47–100%) in Gemma4 reports are driven by both (a) table linearization during HTML stripping causing period misattribution, and (b) genuine hallucinations in forward projections. The HTML table parser resolves (a) but reveals (b): EBITDA margins of 43–72% projected vs. ~9% actual for ETSY/ROKU are not extraction artifacts — they are model-generated errors. Revenue growth rates and margin projections are the primary sources of genuine hallucination across both phases.

  3. Signal quality (with bootstrap CIs and significance tests): Date-gated historical backtesting (27 observations: 9 tickers × 3 cutoff dates, Jun–Dec 2025) shows BUY signals produced +16.5% average 6-month return (95% bootstrap CI: [+6.5%, +25.7%]) vs. −16.4% for HOLD (95% CI: [−28.8%, −4.6%]). Mann-Whitney U test: p = 0.0035 (α < 0.01). Welch’s t-test: p = 0.0006. Cohen’s d = 1.51 (very large effect). NVDA’s Jun 2025 BUY signal generated +62.2% 12-month return, +32.6% alpha.

  4. Critic panel: Phase 5 three-agent critique (Skeptical Analyst, Quant Risk Reviewer, Model Risk Reviewer) identified thesis-changing inaccuracies in all 3 tested Phase 1 reports (stability 0.15–0.25). All 10 tickers receive FAIL or HUMAN_REVIEW gate decisions — the pipeline correctly withholds endorsement at current coverage levels. The model risk reviewer assigns all runs to prototype or conditional-production readiness; production-ready requires SCR ≥ 0.80.

  5. Deployment readiness: The gate decision framework (PASS/HUMAN_REVIEW/FAIL) and IC memo generator provide actionable outputs for a model-risk review board. The experiment manifest captures full reproducibility metadata (git SHA, prompt versions, data sources, fallback flags) for audit trail requirements.

The broader implication: reliability is a harness-level property. The same FinRobot model with a verification layer produces verifiably auditable outputs. The verification scorecard can function as a signal quality filter and a deployment gate. A production deployment would target SCR ≥ 0.80 and ICR ≤ 0.05 — thresholds currently not met by any ticker in the benchmark, motivating continued development of LLM-based extraction and Tier 2 source integration (company IR filings, earnings transcripts).
